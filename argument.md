# CalFresh Hearing Argument

## Working Case Theory

This document develops the argument for the CalFresh administrative hearing concerning the application of the SNAP boarder exclusion to a person with a disability who requires a medically appropriate diet.

This is a working argument. Each legal proposition should be supported by verified primary authority before being presented as established law.

---

## 1. SNAP's Fundamental Purpose

Congress expressly stated the purpose of SNAP in the Food and Nutrition Act.

The program exists to safeguard the health and well-being of the nation's population by raising levels of nutrition among low-income households and increasing their food-purchasing power so they can obtain a more nutritious diet.

This purpose matters when determining whether a requested disability accommodation would fundamentally alter SNAP.

The requested accommodation is not contrary to SNAP's nutritional purpose. It is intended to allow access to food necessary to follow a medically recommended diet.

### Authority

Food and Nutrition Act of 2008, 7 U.S.C. § 2011.

---

## 2. The Boarder Exclusion

The SNAP boarder exclusion originates in federal law rather than merely in a California administrative policy.

Current federal law provides:

> "In no event shall any individual or group of individuals constitute a household if they reside in an institution or boarding house, or else live with others and pay compensation to the others for meals."

### Authority

7 U.S.C. § 2012(n)(4).

The federal SNAP regulations implement this statutory restriction.

Therefore, the argument should not incorrectly characterize the boarder exclusion as merely a discretionary CalFresh policy that California may freely disregard.

---

## 3. The Martin Question: Why Does the Boarder Rule Exist?

The important question is not merely what the boarder exclusion says.

The important question is:

**Why did Congress create it?**

The legislative history of the 1981 change provides unusually direct evidence of congressional purpose.

During consideration of S. 1007, the Congressional Record described the boarder provision as closing a loophole.

Congress explained:

> "If persons pay room and board for their meals, they do not need food stamps since they already receive meals."

The discussion also identified a second concern: a member of an otherwise eligible household might declare himself a boarder and apply separately in order to obtain additional benefits.

Therefore, at least two purposes appear in the legislative history:

1. Congress assumed that people paying room and board were already receiving the meals for which SNAP would otherwise provide purchasing power.

2. Congress sought to prevent manipulation of household status to obtain additional benefits.

### Authority

Congressional Record, June 9, 1981, discussion of S. 1007, "Boarders."

This legislative history must be preserved because it provides evidence of the problem Congress intended the boarder exclusion to solve.

---

## 4. Application of the Martin Question

The requested disability accommodation should be compared with the purposes identified above.

The request does not depend upon pretending that no meals are provided through the room-and-board arrangement.

Meals are provided.

Instead, the issue is whether the congressional assumption underlying the exclusion fully applies when a person with a disability has medically documented nutritional requirements that the provided meals do not adequately permit him to meet.

The question becomes:

**Does allowing supplemental CalFresh purchasing power for medically appropriate food defeat the purposes for which Congress enacted the boarder exclusion?**

That question should be considered separately from the simple fact that the individual technically falls within the ordinary boarder definition.
