# CalFresh Hearing — The Denny Crane Page

## The Rule

Federal SNAP law excludes a person who lives with others and pays them for meals from constituting a separate SNAP household.

I fit the ordinary description of a boarder.

I am not asking the judge to pretend otherwise.

## Why the Rule Exists

When Congress addressed boarders in 1981, it explained its reasoning remarkably plainly: people paying room and board already receive meals, so they do not need food stamps to purchase those meals.

Congress was also concerned about people manipulating household status to obtain additional benefits.

## What Makes This Case Different

I do receive meals.

But I have medically documented dietary requirements related to disability, and the meals provided through my room-and-board arrangement do not adequately permit me to follow the diet recommended by my physician.

The factual assumption underlying the boarder rule — that the provided meals satisfy the need SNAP purchasing power would otherwise address — therefore does not completely fit my circumstances.

## The Disability-Law Question

Title II of the ADA and Section 504 protect people with disabilities in government benefit programs.

Public entities must consider reasonable disability-related modifications unless the modification would change an essential eligibility requirement or fundamentally alter the nature of the program.

And disability is not foreign to SNAP's household rules.

Federal law already permits certain people age 60 or older who cannot purchase and prepare food because of disability to be treated separately for household purposes, subject to statutory conditions.

That provision does not itself override the boarder exclusion.

But it demonstrates something important: individualized disability-related treatment within SNAP's household rules is not a concept foreign to the program.

That creates the real question in this case:

**Can the boarder rule reasonably be modified for one otherwise eligible person whose disability-related nutritional needs cannot adequately be met by the meals provided, without fundamentally altering SNAP?**

## What I Am Asking For

I am not asking to abolish the boarder rule.

I am not claiming that every disabled boarder should receive CalFresh.

I am not asking for reimbursement because a special diet costs more.

And I am not claiming that my residence provides no food.

I am asking for an individualized accommodation: access to CalFresh purchasing power so that I can supplement the meals I receive with food that permits me to follow my medically documented diet.

That request serves SNAP's stated purpose: safeguarding health and improving nutrition by increasing the food-purchasing power of low-income people.

## The Hardest Question

The strongest argument against me is straightforward: Congress itself put the boarder exclusion into federal law.

The government may therefore argue that making an exception would fundamentally alter an essential eligibility requirement.

That is the difficult legal question, and it should not be minimized.

But neither should the disability inquiry disappear simply because the ordinary rule clearly applies to me.

## The Case in Two Sentences

The question for the judge is not whether I am a boarder.

**I am.**

The question is whether disability law requires a reasonable, individualized modification when the very assumption Congress gave for excluding boarders — that their nutritional need is already being met because their meals are provided — does not adequately describe the circumstances of the person sitting before the court.
